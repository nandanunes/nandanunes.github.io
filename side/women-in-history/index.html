Women in History

Women in History is a project developed as senior year thesis at University of Sao Paulo in 2017. It’s an experimental board game that introduces influential women from various backgrounds and their contributions to our society.

Role
Game Design, UX/UI, Visual Designer
Occasion
Senior Thesis
Year
2017

Women in History

So many women have been erased from or forgotten by history. In a Scientific American Mind article “Where are all the female geniuses?” by Upson and Friedman (2012), a simple thought experiment is proposed: name 10 female geniuses from any period of history. “Odds are you run out of names pretty quickly”. Not only in science but in other fields such as history, popular culture and politics it’s possible to notice the gender disparity. That fact causes a negative influence on girls' and women’s self esteem. We have to uncover those who were disappeared from history books if we want to achieve equity in society.

My thesis aims to bring the righteous recognition to the women who I believe were erased from history by the help of an engaging board game. Given that games can be powerful narrative tools, this project is about bringing history and game design together.

What is this project?

I had never heard about Hypatia and her contributions to mathematics, astronomy, philosophy and mechanics or knew that Rosalind Franklin discovered the molecular structure of DNA whereas I had plenty of knowledge about men and their achievements. With little research I came across several studies about women’s representation in school textbooks in historical context. One article in particular grabbed my attention was Voices Not Heard: Women in a History Textbook (1996) by Joyce Delaney. In her studies, she found out that for every six image of women in a textbook, there were 50 others depicting men. All the books used in the research showed strong gender bias. The few pictures and mentions of women were representing domestic activities and subordination towards men. That made me come up with the question that helped make this project real:

So...where were those women I’ve never heard of in my life?

With all this data and motivation I started my project with a clear goal to bring those women to light and introduce them through a fun medium. Women in History is an experimental project where I started the design process with a theme and no idea how to apply on a game mechanic. Throughout the process I experimented different types of games using my theme as a “skin”.

Main goal

When I first started my sketches I didn’t see how all my ideas were leading me to a very complex project. Starting a game project backwards maybe wasn’t the brightest idea.

An issue that I didn’t foresee coming was how the game mechanic terminology could sometimes conflict the storytelling. Such as the action of buying cards could easily be worded as buying “women”.

Introducing Historical Women Figures

There are many women who showed great bravery and brought remarkable innovations to our society. Unfortunately we barely know a dozen of them. These women are from all around the world, carrying all sorts of stories of defying patriarchy and social norms; from Nellie Bly going undercover as an investigative journalist to Kate Sheppard fighting for women's right to vote.

The selection process was tough. I wanted to show diverse fields and achievements. I started the process by selecting the maximum amount of figures I could from fields such as literature, military, science and politics. As much as I wanted to include all notable women in history, I balanced them out by their period of time, race and country of origin.

Positive Representation

The last thing I wanted to do was yet another “Female Fighter Simulator” or a tarot deck. I wanted to introduce those figures in a historical and inspirational point of view. So my next challenge was finding the best game mechanic which would prevent any misrepresentation of women. After analyzing various board games, I found three requirements that were a must in my project:

1. The players should have visual contact with the images of women

Splendor and Machi Koro are great examples as they bring a vivid game space using their cards on the table. So I researched ways to depict the female figures on cards to find the best one.  One idea that stood out was to use real photos of the women selected for the game. Illustrations are an overused way to communicate ideas on these type of games but I considered it due to the ease of production. However, during the tests most of the players said that using real photos of the figures would give more drama and empathy to the game.

2. The players should know what those women did

As they were doctors, soldiers, revolutionaries, scientists,  showing their real photos in a historical context would reinforce the idea that a woman can be anything and anywhere she wants to be. Separating the cards by fields would also communicate their occupation fields.

3. The game shouldn’t use “negative” terms

The most common words and actions in board games are: buy, sell, steal and trade. Considering all the history of oppression women went through (and still go through), those terms would sound inappropriate for a game that wants bring positive representation. Actions such as: buying women cards, stealing from the other player, trading women to get more points… Not such a good idea. I tried my best to find ways to contour those problems and learned a lot during the process. I will explain them in the following part.

Storytelling (and mechanic)

Mechanics are usually the core of a game. But in Women in History, the core is the storytelling. During the process of translating their history into the game I was able to solve some obstacles towards the actions in game terminology.

A set of colors and symbols were assigned to the cards depending on each figure’s profession/field. The symbols were used on the die as well to activate the cards effects.

Another important part of the project was the Achievement cards. They had two important roles in the game: setting the goal in the game, and giving facts and information about the figures. For example:

🏆

Achievement Card: Right to Vote

📖

Text: It is 1918 and now women have the right to vote (at least in the UK)! You can also play with one extra dice.

To obtain the women and Achievement cards, the player has to collect Prestige points. It is sort of the currency of the game and allows the player to gather cards into her pool in order to achieve her “goal”. The word “prestige” was chosen to get rid of any idea of monetization in game. Like said before, the idea of buying women sounds very wrong. This way, the players have to prestige the cards to advance in the game. The character cards generate more prestige points, supporting the idea that they are all working together to help each other.

Conclusion

When I started this project I didn’t imagine what kind of (rewarding) trouble I was getting into. I realized that the problem was not only by starting a project with only a theme in mind – I did a couple of times in Game Jams – but the complexity of the theme. Trying to make a game with such political and moral concepts were particularly challenging.

The game is still not fully completed. The art and mechanics need some adjustments or perhaps a complete redesign. But everything I learned from the process while researching and learning, playtesting with friends, and creating something from scratch was priceless.

I was able to bring together two of my big passions in life: women’s visibility and game design. All the process of building a product that it is going to deliver an important message through a fun game is amazing.

Rules briefly explained

Women in History is a fast-paced game for 2-4 players. Each player needs to promote important women figures in order to unlock achievements related to women's rights. Each player rolls one or two Dice on their turn. If the Symbol of the Die rolled matches the Symbol of a Woman Card that a player owns, she gets the effect of that Card. In some cases opponents will also benefit from your Die (just as you can benefit from theirs). Then a player can unlock an Achievement or a New Character Card with the Prestige Tokens in hand . The first player to unlock all the Achievement Cards ends the game.
