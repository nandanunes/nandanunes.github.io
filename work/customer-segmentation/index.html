Customer Segmentation

Nubank is a Brazilian neobank and the largest fintech in Latin America. Their Credit Card was key to disrupting the Brazilian banking system, bringing innovation both in terms of business and design, becoming an international reference in the field.

Role
Research, UX

I joined Nubank's Credit Card team as a Senior Product Designer, responsible for monitoring and improving the activation flow of the “Underserved” population from the design and customer perspective.

❓

“Underserved” is the name given to clients with low or no credit score. They can't have direct access to a Credit Card – they have to build their way up and prove to be good payers so the financial institutions can trust them.

Overview

My main responsibility was to propose different ways to improve retention in all steps of the Activation funnel, including:

  • Partnering with the Product Manager (PM) to create and track AB tests, measure the impact, and draw new hypotheses;
  • Partnering with the Content Designer (CD) to propose and AB test different copy, improving customer understanding;
  • Partnering with other Product Designers (PD) across different teams and Business Units (BU) such as Checking Accounts, Investments, and App Home, to align design initiatives from the Underserved team;
  • Partnering with Researchers to reach out to customers and get feedback on the product;
  • Leading and following sideline initiatives that would contribute to the product understanding such as improving the Underserved credit card welcome kit;

Besides that, my biggest contribution was leading the Customer Segmentation Research (CSR), an initiative that helped clarify the product team's vision and build a stronger roadmap for the near future of the product so the team could decide to either keep improving the product or start new initiatives.

Process

The CSR process was divided into six steps

  1. Initiative Kickoff – I gathered insights from previous surveys and research; and aligned with the product team to get their feedback on the research;
  2. Exploration Analysis – I partnered with the responsible PM and another PD to sketch and consolidate what behaviors and characteristics seem to influence the funnel;
  3. Survey – Collection of additional information related to customer perceptions, behaviors, and financial life. This survey was inspired by Superhuman’s optimization of Product Marketing Fit. Both quantitative and qualitative data were collected and analyzed.
  4. Profiles Creation – After the consolidation of the results, we tweaked and improved the initial profiles and segments defined in Step 2. That was further validated and improved with quantitative data from the Business Analyst team.
  5. Qualitative Interviews – Once the profiles and behaviors were defined, qualitative data from deep listening would be needed to enrich the profiles to give a better understanding of their pains and needs. Mental models would be drawn from those profiles.
  6. Target Segments and product strategy review – With the mental models in hand, the whole team would have the necessary tools to explore new ways to improve the product and prioritize what needed to be tackled first.
